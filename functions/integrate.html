Integrate

Integrate lets you quantify your spectra: integrate regions, measure signal-to-noise, and analyse DNP, cross-polarisation build-up and offset series.

What it does

Integrate is everything that measures areas. Integration Regions is where you select, calculate and store integrals. The other sections build on integrals for particular experiments: DNP (enhancement, and field-sweep profiles), Signal-to-Noise, CP Build-up and the Offset Tracker for HMQC and J-resolved series.

Before you start

Load and process the spectrum, and select it in Open experiments. Integrals are only as good as the phase and baseline, so check both first.

How to use it

  1. Click Integrate in the toolbar and open Integration Regions.
  2. Define regions. Click Select and Integrate Regions from Plot and drag across each peak, or click Find Automatically (the threshold is set in multiples of the noise σ, or as a percentage of the maximum). Add Region and Remove Region edit the list by hand.
  3. Choose the Integration rule (Trapezoid or Simpson). The areas are calculated and shown.
  4. To keep the numbers, tick the Regions to store and click Store integral in “Stored integrals”. View Stored Integral Table shows everything you have stored.
  5. For a series (for example a variable-contact-time or relaxation experiment), Export Integration to Pseudo-2D… sends the integrals on for curve fitting.

Options and controls

Signal-to-Noise

Spectra
Tick the spectra to measure. By default every spectrum uses the same regions; Different regions for some spectra lets one spectrum have its own, marked in the table and the plot.
Signal (ppm) / Noise (ppm)
Type the signal and noise regions, or click Drag… and drag them on the plot. The noise region must contain no signal; a region that overlaps the signal is refused.
Noise trend removed / Line / Height measured from
How the noise is detrended, whether the line is positive or negative, and where the height is measured from.
Calculate S/N / Full Report… / Copy Report
Gives S/N with an uncertainty, alongside the TopSpin-style and Ph. Eur. values, the integral S/N and S/N per √scan. Hover a row for its formula. Quote the convention with the number: the conventions differ by fixed factors.

DNP

Enhancement (steps 1 to 5)
Work down the steps: tick the spectra (one is the microwaves-off spectrum), pick a reference line so every spectrum is aligned to it, check the alignment, measure the height and/or integral of the line, then Calculate Enhancement as ε = ON / OFF, or 1 − ON / OFF. Measure both the height and the integral if you can: they agree only if the line shape is unchanged.
Field Sweep Profile
Plots signal (absolute, or as enhancement) against B₀ for spectra each recorded at a different field. Plot Profile draws it and Export CSV… saves it.

CP Build-up

Spectra and contact times
Tick a variable-contact-time series and Read Contact Times from an acquisition parameter.
Region and integration
Integrate one region in every spectrum, choosing the baseline treatment and whether to divide by the receiver gain, then Integrate and Plot.
Fit the build-up
Fits the integral against contact time with a choice of models, from the classical I–S model (with TIS and T1ρ(I)) to two-component and non-classical models. Starting Values from Data and Fit All Regions help with the set-up.

Offset Tracker

Spectra and offsets
For 1D spectra of one nucleus recorded while pulsing on another at a stepped offset (sideband-selective HMQC, J-resolved): integrate the same region of every spectrum and plot it against the offset. Read Offsets takes them from the acquisition parameters.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Trapezoid or Simpson? They agree on well-sampled data. If they differ noticeably, your regions contain too few points; zero-fill more in Process.
  • The noise region must be signal-free. Choose one well away from every peak and from any baseline roll at the edges.
  • Comparing S/N between spectra. Use the same regions for every spectrum, and note that S/N should scale with the square root of the number of scans.
  • Overlapping peaks. If peaks are too overlapped to integrate separately, fit them in Fit instead and use the fitted areas.
