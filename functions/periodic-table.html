Look up the NMR properties of any nucleus and its Larmor frequency at your magnetic field.

What it does

The Periodic Table is a quick reference for the nuclei you can observe. Click an element to see its NMR-active isotopes (spin, natural abundance, gyromagnetic ratio, quadrupole moment and receptivity), and read off the Larmor frequency of any nucleus at your field. It also lists every nucleus at that field, which helps when you are choosing a reference or checking whether two nuclei can be observed on the same channel.

Before you start

Nothing. It works with no spectrum loaded. If you do have one selected, From selected spectrum takes the field from it.

How to use it

  1. Click Periodic Table in the toolbar.
  2. Set the magnet. Pick a Spectrometer preset (60 MHz benchtop up to 850 MHz), type the field (Field B₀) or the ¹H frequency, or click From selected spectrum.
  3. Choose a nucleus by clicking an element on the table, typing in Find (a symbol, name, atomic number or a nuclide such as 195Pt), or using the Nucleus list.
  4. Read the Larmor frequency, with the working shown. Copy copies it.
  5. Open All nuclei at this field to see every nucleus listed by frequency.

Options and controls

Spin I
The coloured buttons above the table highlight elements whose most abundant NMR isotope has that spin. The bar on each tile is that colour, and the number in its corner is how many NMR isotopes the element has.
Tiles show
Chooses what the bottom line of each tile displays: atomic weight, Larmor frequency (MHz), receptivity (relative to ¹³C), or the principal isotope and spin.
Find
Searches by symbol, name, atomic number or nuclide.
Nucleus panel
Spin, natural abundance, γ/2π, quadrupole moment Q (not defined for I = ½) and receptivity relative to ¹³C and ¹H.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • These are bare-nucleus frequencies. The Larmor frequency shown is |γ/2π| × B₀. It is not the IUPAC Ξ reference frequency, so it can differ slightly from the frequency at which your spectrometer observes a referenced signal.
  • The same data are used throughout openNMR. The nuclear data here are the same table openNMR uses when it references spectra.
  • Radioactive isotopes. ³H and ⁹⁹Tc are not naturally present; their tabulated abundance is 0%.
