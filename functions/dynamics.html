Dynamics

Dynamics is for fitting pseudo-2D data (relaxation and diffusion) and for exploring how mobility changes a spectrum.

What it does

Dynamics is where a series of spectra becomes a rate or a diffusion coefficient. It fits relaxation (T1, T2, T1ρ) and diffusion (PFG) curves from a pseudo-2D series, and its sections go on from there: Batch processing of many series (for example T1 at every temperature), Relaxation vs Temperature (BPP and Arrhenius analysis), Exchange Lineshape (ion hopping) and Regularisation (distributions of T1, T2 or D).

Before you start

Select a pseudo-2D series in Open experiments (or a stack of separately loaded 1D spectra), and phase it first. Then measure each slice in Fit (fit the peaks, or define integration regions in Integrate). Dynamics fits the curve those values make.

How to use it

  1. Select the pseudo-2D series in Open experiments, and phase it in Process.
  2. In Fit, fit the peaks across the series (or define integration regions). This gives one intensity per peak per slice.
  3. In Dynamics, choose the Fit type (Relaxation, or Diffusion (PFG)) and the Model, for example Inversion recovery (T1) or Exponential decay (T2).
  4. Check where the x-values come from under Delays (see below), and that the Unit is right.
  5. Click Fit Curve…. The fitted curve, its parameters and their uncertainties are shown, with What the curve fit tells you, and what to try next.
  6. Keep a fit with Keep This Curve Fit…, or Export curve fit (CSV)….

Options and controls

Fit Curve settings

Fit type
Relaxation fits against delay (from the experiment's vdlist, or typed). Diffusion fits a Stejskal–Tanner model against b-value.
Model
Inversion or saturation recovery, exponential or biexponential decay, stretched (Kohlrausch–Williams–Watts) forms for a distribution of times, or your own Equation. Components sets how many.
Value source
What is fitted from each slice: the fitted peak area (the default, and the most robust to a linewidth that changes along the series), the fitted peak amplitude, or an integrated region with no fit.
Delays
Where the x-values come from: Auto (the experiment's own vdlist or diff.xml), Manual list, Start + step, List file × factor, Relative gradient, or b from gradients (δ/Δ/G). The line under it always shows what the current source resolves to.
Diffusion settings (δ, Δ, τ, gradient)
The gradient pulse and diffusion time, the maximum gradient for your probe, the Gradient shape and the Sequence. Read δ/Δ from file fills in what the experiment records. Save gradient for probe remembers the maximum gradient for this probe.
Starting values
Override the automatic starting guess, bounds and fixed/free state for each parameter. Adopt last fit restarts from the previous result.
Fit all peaks together
Fits every peak simultaneously with some parameters shared. Use it when the peaks belong to one species and must share one T1, T2 or D.
Slices used in fits
Untick a slice to drop it from every fit on this spectrum.
Resolve Components (SCORE/OUTSCORE)…
Fits the whole data matrix at once as a sum of components, each with its own spectrum and decay constant. This can separate species whose peaks overlap, which no per-peak fit can do.
Show Parameter Correlations
Shows the correlation matrix of the fitted parameters instead of the curve.

The other sections

Batch
Fits a whole set of pseudo-2D series in one go, for example one T1 measurement per temperature. Set up one series by hand as the reference, process every series the same way, fit them all, then Send the results to Relaxation vs Temperature.
Relaxation vs Temperature
Plots T1, T2, T1ρ or linewidth against temperature (as ln(T) against 1000/T) and fits a BPP model for the activation energy and correlation time (Arrhenius or VFT), dipolar or quadrupolar, with asymmetric (β < 2) forms for correlated hopping. Linewidths are fitted with the Hendrickson–Bray equation.
Exchange Lineshape (Ion Hopping)
Simulates spectra of nuclei hopping between sites from the Bloch–McConnell equations. You define the sites and the (reversible) hops and the temperatures, and can fit the model to the spectra selected in Open experiments.
Regularisation
Inverts a series into a distribution of T1, T2 or D (or a 2D map) rather than a few discrete components, using Tikhonov regularisation.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Use real b-values for diffusion. openNMR will not run DOSY or SCORE if the x-axis has fallen back to plain slice numbers, because the resulting diffusion coefficients would carry no physical unit. Read them from a Bruker diff.xml, or supply δ, Δ and the gradient.
  • Get the gradient shape right. A stock Bruker sequence is almost always the smoothed square (SMSQ10.100). Treating it as a perfect rectangle overestimates b by 23%, which lands directly on D.
  • Sample the whole decay. A component that loses only 20% of its intensity across the series is nearly constant, and its decay constant is poorly determined however good the fit looks.
  • Fit quality does not validate a multi-component resolution. Very different answers can reproduce the data equally well. Check the cross-talk and the warnings the report gives, not just R².
  • Overlapping peaks. If peaks are too overlapped to fit, integrate regions instead and fit those.
  • Notebook. NMR2D_relaxation_workflow runs the whole relaxation pipeline in one call, reading delays from the experiment's vdlist.
