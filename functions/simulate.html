Simulate

Simulate synthesises spectra from parameters you choose, so you can see what a model predicts before you fit it, or compare a calculation against your data.

What it does

Simulate is a forward calculator: you give it the physics and it gives you the spectrum. There is a simulator for each situation: Solution NMR (strongly coupled spin systems, solved exactly), Solid-state NMR, formerly CSA, Quad & J-coupling (powder patterns and multiplets, static or MAS), Czjzek distribution (disordered quadrupolar sites), DFT tensors (from CASTEP, VASP, ORCA, Gaussian or CRYSTAL calculations), Diffusion (PFG), Shaped pulse (Bloch) and pNMR (CIF). Results can be kept as spectra and compared directly with real data.

Before you start

Nothing needs to be loaded: Simulate works on its own. If a spectrum is open, simulations can use its nucleus and field, and you can keep the result next to your data in Open experiments.

How to use it

  1. Click Simulate in the toolbar and open the simulator you want, for example CSA, Quad & J-coupling.
  2. Choose the Nucleus and the field (Larmor frequency or Field B₀), and whether the sample is a Static powder or under Finite MAS (then set the MAS rate).
  3. Add one or more sites and give each its parameters (see below). Add interactions (CSA, quadrupolar coupling, J-couplings) to each site as needed.
  4. Click Simulate. The pattern is drawn in the Simulation tab.
  5. Click Keep in Open Experiments to turn the simulation into a spectrum you can compare, fit against or export, or Send to Fit as Starting Values to start a fit from it.

Options and controls

CSA, Quad & J-coupling

Convention
Haeberlen (Δ, η) or Herzfeld–Berger (span, skew) for the CSA. Each site then has δiso, Span or Δ and Skew or η.
Sites — Add, Duplicate, Remove
One row per site, each with a Label, an Amount (relative population) and its own broadening.
Broadening
Gaussian, Lorentzian or pseudo-Voigt, with a FWHM for each site.
Powder averaging / Orientations
ZCW, rectangular (θ/φ) or Lebedev, and how many orientations to use. More orientations are slower but smoother.
Axis
Auto frames the pattern (δiso ± 1.5 × |span or Δ|); or set the minimum, maximum and number of points yourself.
Quadrupolar sites
Choose the Transitions (central transition, satellites, or both), the Method (first order, second order, or exact diagonalisation), and CQ and ηQ. Compare orders overlays first order, second order and exact.
J-coupled sites
Give each site coupling groups (the number of equivalent partners, their nucleus and J). Lines from partners' other isotopes appear as satellites with the right natural-abundance weights. Strongly coupled peaks solves a spin system exactly for strong coupling.
Tensor picture (3D)
Show Tensors draws the interaction tensor as a frequency surface or as an ellipsoid with axes.

Other simulators

SIMPSON import
Brings the output of a SIMPSON simulation into openNMR, in four steps. 1. SIMPSON files: add the files (Add files…). 2. Spectrometer: say what the file holds (spectrum or FID; the default is to detect it), the nuclide and the field (or the ¹H frequency), so the axis can be in ppm; with no nuclide the axis stays in Hz. 3. Spectra and FIDs: Plot them, or Add to Open experiments to process, compare and fit them like measured data. An FID is transformed here and stays an FID, so the window, zero-filling and phase act on it live. A file that holds several spectra or FIDs (NELEM > 1) offers an Element to plot. Send to Fit sends a spectrum on to Fit ▸ SIMPSON spectrum. 4. Points from FIDs: reads the first points of every file (A = first point, B = second, …) and plots them, or an expression such as A + B - 0.3*C, against a quantity you name (the number in each file name, a start and step, or values you type). Plot points, Save table (CSV)… or Copy notebook code for the same calculation in a notebook. Scan tables (a file of x, y and z columns) are drawn as a map or as lines, and .tcl input scripts fill in the nuclide and field boxes without being run.
Czjzek distribution
The powder spectrum of a site whose quadrupolar coupling is spread over a Czjzek distribution, with the distribution of (CQ, ηQ) shown beside it. Choose plain Czjzek (a random field gradient centred on zero, set by σ alone) or Extended Czjzek (a mean field gradient plus a random part).
DFT tensors
Reads a CASTEP .magres file, VASP OUTCAR, ORCA output, Gaussian log or CRYSTAL output, plus one or more reference compounds with their experimental shifts. openNMR fits a shift scale from the references, converts calculated shieldings to shifts, and simulates the spectrum. Choose solid or solution state, and average sites that are equivalent in the real material.
DFT tensors: CRYSTAL (paramagnetic)
A CRYSTAL calculation is the fully spin-polarised state, so the box Paramagnetic shift scaling (CRYSTAL) scales its hyperfine interactions to your sample's magnetisation. Give the scaling directly, or from a molar susceptibility and the spin, or from a Curie–Weiss θ, μeff and spin at a temperature and field (B₀ from the Larmor frequency fills the field). Choose Fermi contact, dipolar or both, and add an orbital shift. Atoms are named by element and CRYSTAL atom number (for example Na15). Only the hyperfine shift is calculated, so the orbital part must be supplied. A scaling typed in belongs to the field it was calculated for. Copy Import as Python gives the same steps for a notebook.
Diffusion (PFG)
Simulates a diffusion decay for a chosen D, gradient shape and pulse sequence (spin echo, stimulated echo, bipolar). It can compare gradient shapes or sequence types.
Shaped pulse (Bloch)
Calculates the excitation profile of a shaped pulse (Gaussian, sinc, BURP, hyperbolic secant, WURST and others) against offset, for a given power.
pNMR (CIF)
Predicts the spectrum of a paramagnetic material from a crystal structure. Load a CIF, choose the species and sites to include, and define shells of neighbours with a shift contribution for each.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Which quadrupolar method? First order is fast but only right when CQ is small compared with the Larmor frequency. Use Compare orders to see where first order, second order and exact diagonalisation part company for your parameters.
  • MAS sidebands. Under MAS, every spinning sideband is included. A fast MAS rate narrows the central transition but does not remove its second-order lineshape.
  • Natural abundance matters. A coupling to a 1% abundant partner appears as tiny satellites, whereas a coupling to a ~100% abundant partner changes the whole multiplet.
  • Notebook. Panels have Copy notebook code (or Copy as Python) buttons, so a simulation set up here can be reproduced in a notebook.
