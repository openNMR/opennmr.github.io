Advanced processing

Advanced processing gives you specialised tools for more demanding data: wide-line spectra recorded at several offsets, CPMG echo trains, 2D shearing, and the aMAT spectral filter.

What it does

Advanced processing holds methods that go beyond the usual FID-to-spectrum steps in Process. There are four, each in its own section:

  • Variable Offset Cumulative Spectroscopy (VOCS) corrects and combines spectra recorded at several transmitter offsets, to cover a line broader than one pulse can excite uniformly.
  • CPMG co-adds the echoes of a CPMG echo train into a single echo and Fourier-transforms it, for a large gain in signal-to-noise on broad lines.
  • Shearing and Scaling (2D) shears and scales a 2D spectrum so an anisotropic ridge lies along an axis (MQMAS, STMAS, DAS), or tilts a J-resolved or sideband spectrum by 45°.
  • aMAT Spectral Filter (2D) removes, from an aMAT spectrum, the artefacts of intense signals that hide weak ones at fast MAS, by keeping each F1 row only at its own isotropic shift plus whole multiples of the spinning rate.

Before you start

Load the data with Open and select it. VOCS Sum works on several spectra at once: select a series, or tick them in Compare. CPMG needs a Bruker CPMG experiment. Shearing needs a 2D spectrum. The aMAT filter needs a processed aMAT 2D whose F1 is the isotropic shift on the same ppm scale as F2.

How to use it

VOCS

  1. Process each offset spectrum in Process, then click Adv. Proc. in the toolbar and open VOCS.
  2. Enter the Pulse param settings for the pulse used. Tick Limit to VOCS excitation bandwidth to zero the correction outside the pulse's useful bandwidth, instead of amplifying noise far from the centre.
  3. Select the series (or tick it in Compare) and click VOCS Sum (selected spectra) or VOCS Sum (Compare stack). The result is a new spectrum on one common ppm axis, with intensities preserved. Apply VOCS to Selected Spectrum corrects a single spectrum.

CPMG

  1. Select a Bruker CPMG experiment and open CPMG.
  2. Check the Pulse-program values (echo length, blanking delay, number of echoes). Then click Check echo split: it draws where the echoes will be cut. If the split is wrong, untick Auto and give the First point yourself.
  3. Choose which echoes to co-add (Skip first echoes, Echoes to co-add), set the apodisation and zero-filling, and click Process CPMG.

Shearing

  1. Select a 2D spectrum and open Shearing and Scaling (2D).
  2. Choose the Experiment (MQMAS, STMAS, DAS, 2D J-resolved, 2D PASS, MAT, shifting-p echo or shifting-d echo) and its settings (spin, MQ order, pathway). Tick Acquired with split-t1 if the data arrive already sheared.
  3. Choose the F1 scaling. Click Check against the data to measure the shear that gives the sharpest projection and compare it with the standard value, then Preview.
  4. Click Shear → New Spectrum. The result is added to Open experiments with a log of what was done.

aMAT spectral filter

  1. Select a processed aMAT 2D and open aMAT Spectral Filter (2D). The MAS rate is read from the experiment when it was recorded.
  2. Set the Pulse width (a fraction of νrot, νrot/3 by default, or in kHz) and the Pulse shape. To have the width checked, give T2′.
  3. Click Show Filter to see the 2D filter: the red ridge of centre pulses should lie on the diagonal. Set Trace at F1 to a weak site and click Show Trace to see its row before and after, with the pulse train above it.
  4. Click Filter → New Spectrum. The filtered 2D is added to Open experiments with a log of the settings and how much was removed. Copy as Python gives the same filter for a notebook.

Options and controls

VOCS Sum
Corrects and combines every selected (or Compare-ticked) spectrum onto one common ppm axis. No need to apply VOCS to each spectrum first.
CPMG echo split
First point, Auto offset (points) and Safety (points) control where the echoes are cut.
CPMG processing
Apodise the co-added echo, Zeroed edge points, Window shape, Window alpha, Zero-fill to. Untick Magnitude mode to keep the real and imaginary parts and phase the result in Process.
Shear: Shifting-p and shifting-d echo
Presets for the p-echo and d-echo (and the adiabatic double and quadruple d-echo, which has the same geometry). One shear parallel to t1 separates the shift from the first-order quadrupolar pattern. In the p-echo F1 holds the shift and F2 the quadrupolar pattern about zero; in the d-echo it is the other way round. The whole echo must be acquired, with t2 starting at the end of the last pulse and no echo alignment. Use Check against the data on your own data to confirm the F1 direction.
Projections: Sum or Max
Right-click the plot and choose Projections computed as, or use the selector in the Shear preview. A shear parallel to t1 leaves the F1 sum projection unchanged and alters F2, and the reverse for t2.
Shear: Experiment / Spin / MQ order / Pathway / Satellite
Describe the experiment. The spin is set from the selected spectrum's nucleus when it is quadrupolar.
Shear: F1 scaling
Chemical-shift scale (δF1 = δCS − 10/17 δQIS), Unified, 1 + |κ| (Grandinetti), no scaling (sheared Hz), or a custom factor.
Shear: F1 direction
Whether F1 is displayed with the same sense as the coherence evolving in t1 or reversed. It depends on the quadrature scheme. Check against the data sets it.
Shear: Second shear
Also shears parallel to t1, removing the isotropic part from F2 so each site's anisotropic pattern is centred on zero.
Shear: Mark expected sideband positions
For PASS and MAT, marks where sidebands should fall in the preview, given the MAS rate.
aMAT: how the filter works
In aMAT the indirect dimension is modulated by the isotropic shift only, so the row at an isotropic shift ν1 can carry real signal only at ν2 = ν1 + nνrot: its centreband and spinning sidebands. Each row is multiplied by a train of pulses, one at each of those positions; together they form a 2D filter whose centre pulses lie on the diagonal ν1 = ν2. Real and imaginary parts are filtered alike, so the phase is untouched. After the Supporting Information, section S7, of Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2024, e202408704.
aMAT: Pulse width and shape
Narrower pulses remove more artefacts and noise, but they must stay broader than the homogeneous linewidth 1/(πT2′), or real signal is cut away. Hann-shaped pulses (the default, as in the reference) taper to zero at their edges and emphasise the signal at their centre; their half-height width, half the pulse width, is what is checked against the linewidth. Flat pulses keep everything inside them unchanged.
aMAT: F1 offset
Moves every pulse train relative to its row, for an F1 axis referenced or scaled a little differently from F2. Use it if the ridge in Show Filter is off the diagonal of your data.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • VOCS amplifies noise far from each centre. Keep Limit to VOCS excitation bandwidth on unless you have a reason not to.
  • CPMG echoes cut in the wrong place? Always use Check echo split before processing, and adjust the first point if the echoes are misaligned. Co-adding misaligned echoes blurs the spectrum.
  • Sheared spectrum looks wrong? The F1 direction is the usual cause. Use Check against the data, which measures the shear from your spectrum and sets the direction.
  • aMAT filter cutting a real peak? Widen the pulses, or check with Show Filter that the ridge lies on your data's diagonal; if not, set the F1 offset. The filter cannot remove distortions that fall at a site's own positions.
  • Notebook. CPMGprocess is the notebook equivalent of the CPMG section; nmr.apply_amat_filter, nmr.plot_amat_trace and nmr.plot_amat_filter_map are those of the aMAT filter.
