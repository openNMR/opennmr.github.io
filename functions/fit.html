Fit

Fit finds the parameters that best describe your spectrum, and tells you how far to trust them: peak shapes, anisotropic powder patterns, J-coupled multiplets, disordered sites and more.

What it does

Fit works on spectra: it adjusts a model until it matches your data, and reports every value with its uncertainty. Its sections, in the order the tool lists them: Solution NMR (pseudo-Voigt peaks and first-order multiplets, or an exactly solved strongly coupled spin system); Solid-state NMR (peaks with any mix of CSA, quadrupolar and J-couplings, static or MAS); Quadrupolar (Czjzek distribution) for disordered sites; pNMR (CIF bond pathways) for paramagnetic materials, shown when Paramagnetic NMR is switched on; SIMPSON spectrum, with SIMPSON simulations as templates; Multi-spectrum fits; and Kept fit models, where fits you have saved are compared. (To fit relaxation or diffusion curves, use Dynamics.)

After a fit the panel shows What the fit tells you, and what to try next. It warns about parameters sitting on a bound, strongly correlated pairs, a clipped fit window and a poor R², so that a good-looking curve is not mistaken for a good answer.

Before you start

Load and process the spectrum first: phase it in absorption mode and correct the baseline. Select it in Open experiments. For a pseudo-2D series, Fit works on the slice you choose.

How to use it

For a first fit of peak shapes:

  1. Click Fit in the toolbar, open Solution NMR and choose the model Peaks and multiplets (first order: d, t, q …). (These pseudo-Voigt peaks suit any spectrum of resolved peaks, solid-state MAS included.)
  2. Add peaks: Auto-Pick Peaks finds them for you (Min. prominence sets how small a peak may be), Pick from Plot lets you click them, and Add Peak adds one by hand. Edit the Shift, FWHM and Eta (the pseudo-Voigt mixing) in the table; tick Fixed to hold a value.
  3. To fit only part of the spectrum, define regions in Integrate and tick Fit only inside the regions.
  4. Click Fit Spectrum. The fit is drawn over the data in the Spectral Fit tab and the report appears below, with each parameter and its 1σ error.
  5. Read What the fit tells you, and what to try next. When you are happy, Export Fit Report…, Save the Fitted Values… or Keep This Fit… to store it with the spectrum.

For anisotropic or quadrupolar lineshapes, open Solid-state NMR (called CSA, Quad & J-coupling before version 0.7). Build the model as peaks (sites) with Add peak, then Add interaction ▾ to each: a chemical-shift anisotropy, a quadrupolar coupling, and any number of J-couplings, in any combination, static or MAS. Use Estimate Starting Values to read starting parameters off the data, Preview Starting Model to check the model before fitting, and then Fit Solid-state NMR. A model built in Simulate arrives here with Send to Fit as Starting Values.

Options and controls

Solution NMR

Model
Peaks and multiplets (first order: d, t, q …) is the peak table below: pseudo-Voigt peaks, each optionally a first-order multiplet, right when every shift difference is much larger than J. Strongly coupled spin system (exact: roofing, AB, ABX …) solves the spin system exactly (roofing, AB, ABX and so on) and fits the shifts and J-couplings directly.
One spin system
There is a single strongly-coupled-spin-system panel. It sits in whichever of Solution NMR or Solid-state NMR you used last, and the other section tells you where it went, with a Show it here button.
Peaks (pseudo-Voigt)
Add Peak, Remove Peak and Clear All edit the peak table: Shift, FWHM and Eta (the Lorentzian fraction) for each peak, with Fixed to hold a value. A peak can carry first-order J-couplings to make it a multiplet.
Auto-Pick Peaks / Pick from Plot
Find peaks automatically, or click them on the plot. Pick on slice chooses which slice of a pseudo-2D to pick from.
Fit only inside the regions
Restricts the fit to your integration regions.
Bound peak amplitudes
Bound peak amplitudes by region integrals keeps each peak's amplitude consistent with the integral of its region.
Across a pseudo-2D series
A relaxation or diffusion series is the same peaks measured repeatedly, so only their intensity should change. Fix shift & FWHM from one slice fits one good Anchor slice and holds position and width for the rest; the alternatives fit every slice independently or seed each from the previous one.
Advanced (fit tolerance and S/N)
S/N threshold, noise region, position tolerance and minimum prominence for automatic picking.
Multiplets and J couplings
Reads each multiplet as a first-order pattern and gives its name (s, d, t, q, dd, td, sept …), centre and J values. Find the lines in: Automatic (your integration regions if there are any; otherwise around each line in the peak table, so one picked line per multiplet is enough; otherwise the whole spectrum), or choose one of those, or The picked lines only when you have picked every line yourself. A line counts when it stands 5× the noise above the local baseline and is at least 2 % of its multiplet's tallest line, which keeps a septet's outer lines and leaves out ¹³C satellites. Lines within is the largest gap between lines of one multiplet (about 20 Hz for ¹H; more for ¹⁹F or ³¹P), Tolerance how far a line may sit from where the couplings put it. The pattern is chosen by how well its predicted intensities match the heights above the baseline, so a slight roof does not spoil it, and the J values are refined against every line. A multiplet that is not first order is reported as m with its range. Analyse Multiplets, then Copy Report for the line a paper quotes (¹H NMR (400 MHz) δ 3.45 (dd, J = 10.1, 4.0 Hz), …), and Use in the Fit to replace each multiplet's lines in the peak table with one multiplet row that Fit Spectrum refines. Draw the coupling trees on the plot shows each tree above its multiplet. Phase the spectrum first; beside a far larger peak, draw a tight region round the multiplet. In a notebook: from opennmr.notebook import multiplet_analysis as ma, then results = ma.analyse_spectrum(spectrum, regions=…), nmr.format_multiplet_report(results, "1H", mhz), nmr.plot_coupling_trees(ax, results).
Fit Spectrum
Fit Spectrum runs the fit. A long fit shows its progress; Stop and Keep Best ends it and shows the best parameters reached (without uncertainties, as it had not converged: fit again to carry on), and Discard ends it and changes nothing. A pseudo-2D series stopped part-way is not kept as the fit, but the peak table starts from the slice reached. Show Parameter Correlations shows the correlation matrix of the fitted parameters. Values near ±1 mean two parameters trade off against each other.

Solid-state NMR

Model
Peaks with interactions (CSA, quadrupolar, J to other nuclei), or Strongly coupled peaks (exact J between them), the spin-system fit shared with Solution NMR.
Peaks (sites)
Add peak, Duplicate, Remove: one row per site. Add isotope partner adds a second isotope of the same chemistry (for example 1H and 2H in a multi-spectrum fit); Same chemistry as makes a peak follow another's parameters, except those ticked under Not shared.
Interactions
Add interaction ▾ adds a CSA, a quadrupolar coupling or a J-coupling to the selected peak (a peak with none is an isotropic peak); Copy to Peaks ▾ gives other peaks this peak's interactions, with or without its broadening. The fields are those of Simulate ▸ Solid-state NMR.
Nucleus, Spin I, field
The nucleus and field. The Larmor frequency can be typed, or taken from the spectrum.
Regime, MAS rate
Static powder, or MAS (centreband + sidebands). Fit anisotropic interactions with at least 3 to 5 spinning sidebands for accuracy.
Theory, Transitions
Second-order perturbation, exact diagonalisation, or first-order only; all transitions, the central transition only, or the satellites only.
Orientations / Powder averaging
The number of orientations, with a coarser setting for fitting and a finer one for the final result, and the powder-average scheme: Automatic (the default: ZCW for a static pattern; under MAS, Lebedev for a peak without a quadrupolar interaction and ZCW for one with), ZCW, Lebedev or Rectangular (θ/φ). The fit log records it.
Run time
A fit that could take more than a minute asks before it starts, with an estimated range (fits need between a handful and a few dozen iterations, which cannot be known in advance) and what would make it faster: fewer orientations to start with, fixing what you already know, or screening several starts at fewer orientations. The result reports how long it took next to the estimate.
Parameters
One row per parameter: Start, Free, Min, Max and Try starts. A blank bound means the natural limit. Right-click selected cells to free or fix them, or set their limits and starts together. Try starts takes extra starting values (comma-separated): the fit is run from each and the best is kept, which guards against the wrong minimum of a static powder pattern. Screen starts at (off by default) fits every start with a coarser powder set and refits only the best with the full orientations, which is much quicker.
Fit window
The whole spectrum, a ppm range, several ppm ranges, or your integration regions. Fit the baseline too fits a baseline with the model.
Starting values
Estimate Starting Values reads starting values from the data in the fit window: peaks by peak picking, a CSA or quadrupolar site from the pattern's centre of mass and shape, and, if ticked, the amounts. Undo restores the values you had. Preview Starting Model draws the model over the data before fitting, and Live preview of the starting model redraws it as you edit.
Fit Solid-state NMR
Runs the fit and reports each value with its 1σ error; Show Parameter Correlations shows which pairs trade off. Use fitted values as start copies the result into the starting values for the next model.
Send to Simulate
Sends the fitted model to Simulate as a starting point.

Other sections

Quadrupolar (Czjzek distribution)
Fits Czjzek (or extended Czjzek) distributions of the quadrupolar coupling to the selected 1D spectrum. For disordered sites in glasses and amorphous solids. Show Fit draws the fit; Show Distribution Map draws the fitted (CQ, ηQ) distribution as a map beside the data, fit and sites. Send to Simulate passes the sites on.
pNMR (CIF bond pathways)
Fits a paramagnetic spectrum with the environments predicted from a crystal structure (CIF) and its bond-pathway shifts. The structural model is set up in Simulate ▸ Paramagnetic NMR tools ▸ pNMR (CIF). Shown only when Paramagnetic NMR is switched on in Settings ▸ Sections shown. It runs in three steps: 1. Structural model (Set up in Simulate ▸ pNMR…, or Load settings…); 2. Maximal fit, one peak per group of environments, with the lineshape, FWHM, how far each shift may move and a minimum S/N; and optionally 3. Simplify and refit, which merges low-area peaks into their neighbours or onto shifts you choose. The predicted environments can be drawn as sticks, shown under the cursor, and used to label each peak with its most likely environment. Save environments (CSV)… and Copy notebook code.
SIMPSON spectrum
Fits a measured spectrum as a sum of SIMPSON simulations, used as fixed lineshape templates. Send a spectrum from Simulate ▸ SIMPSON import, or add one here.
  1. 1. SIMPSON spectra: Add selected experiment or Add SIMPSON file… builds the list of templates; Remove takes one out.
  2. 2. Fit: Each template always gets its own amplitude, and a baseline is fitted with them. Tick Each template may shift (with a Shift window ±) to allow for referencing or isotropic-shift differences, and Each template may broaden to add Lorentzian and Gaussian broadening. These two widths are partly degenerate, and the errors say so when they are. Amplitudes cannot be negative and Only inside the Integration regions restrict the fit. Fit runs it, and Copy notebook code gives the same call for a notebook.
  3. 3. Result: A table of each template's amplitude, shift, Lorentzian and Gaussian broadening, each with its uncertainty, and its share of the total.
Multi-spectrum
Fits one set of sites to several spectra consistently: a series (VT or time), each seeded from the previous fit or from the starting model; isotopes of one material (for example 1H and 2H); or one isotope at several fields. Add the experiments (Add selected or Add by number…) and put them in fitting order; Copy sites from another fit. Site amounts can be free per experiment or a ratio shared by all (or per nucleus); each spectrum's baseline, and its MAS rate within a range, can be fitted too. You choose what the x value of each spectrum is (temperature, time since the first, an acquisition parameter, the experiment number or the order). Fit All, then Plot a fitted quantity against x (with Copy Data (CSV)), Keep Fits in Open Experiments… or Copy as Python.
Kept fit models
Lists the fits you kept with Keep This Fit…, so you can compare several models of the same spectrum (for example 2-, 3- and 4-site models). Show one, or tick several and Compare Ticked, with or without their residuals. A kept fit can be restored to its panel, renamed, recoloured, saved (Save the Fitted Values…) or sent to Open Experiments as a spectrum.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Start simple. Fit the isotropic peaks first, then use Use Fitted Values as Start in the next model. A good start matters more than a long fit.
  • A MAS spectrum does not determine everything. At fast MAS the central transition fixes δiso, CQ, ηQ, J and the site ratio precisely, but hardly the CSA, because the sidebands hold under 1% of the intensity. Fix the CSA there. A static (or slow-MAS) spectrum determines the CSA once the quadrupolar parameters are held at their MAS values. This two-step route is the standard experimental strategy and the fit should follow it.
  • Static powder lineshapes have several minima. A fit started from the wrong sign of the anisotropy can settle in a wrong minimum. The panel's Try starts option fits from several starting points and keeps the best.
  • The sign of CQ is not in a lineshape: it depends on CQ², so |CQ| is reported. When a central transition is poorly resolved, δiso and CQ are hard to separate. The report gives the centre of gravity and PQ = CQ√(1 + ηQ²/3), which are well determined even then; a second field separates the two.
  • A good R² is not proof. Read the warnings in the report, and check Show Parameter Correlations for pairs that trade off.
  • In a notebook. The same fitters are available as NMRCSAFitWorkflow, NMRQuadFitWorkflow, NMRJCouplingFitWorkflow and fit_czjzek_spectrum. Several panels have a Copy notebook code button.
