Fit

Fit finds the parameters that best describe your spectrum, and tells you how far to trust them: peak shapes, anisotropic powder patterns, J-coupled multiplets, disordered sites and more.

What it does

Fit works on spectra: it adjusts a model until it matches your data, and reports every value with its uncertainty. Each kind of model has its own section: Isotropic (Gauss/Lor) peaks; CSA, Quad & J-coupling sites built from any mix of interactions; Quadrupolar (Czjzek distribution) for disordered sites; pNMR (CIF bond pathways) for paramagnetic materials; Multi-spectrum fits; and Kept fit models, where fits you have saved are compared. (To fit relaxation or diffusion curves, use Dynamics.)

After a fit the panel shows What the fit tells you, and what to try next. It warns about parameters sitting on a bound, strongly correlated pairs, a clipped fit window and a poor R², so that a good-looking curve is not mistaken for a good answer.

Before you start

Load and process the spectrum first: phase it in absorption mode and correct the baseline. Select it in Open experiments. For a pseudo-2D series, Fit works on the slice you choose.

How to use it

For a first fit of peak shapes:

  1. Click Fit in the toolbar and open Isotropic (Gauss/Lor).
  2. Add peaks: Auto-Pick Peaks finds them for you (Min. prominence sets how small a peak may be), Pick from Plot lets you click them, and Add Peak adds one by hand. Edit the Shift, FWHM and Eta (the pseudo-Voigt mixing) in the table; tick Fixed to hold a value.
  3. To fit only part of the spectrum, define regions in Integrate and tick Fit only inside the regions.
  4. Click Fit Spectrum. The fit is drawn over the data in the Spectral Fit tab and the report appears below, with each parameter and its 1σ error.
  5. Read What the fit tells you, and what to try next. When you are happy, Export Fit Report…, Save the Fitted Values… or Keep This Fit… to store it with the spectrum.

For anisotropic or quadrupolar lineshapes, open CSA, Quad & J-coupling. Build the model as peaks (sites), then add interactions to each peak: a chemical-shift anisotropy, a quadrupolar coupling, and any number of J-couplings, in any combination, static or MAS. Use Estimate Starting Values to read starting parameters off the data, Preview Starting Model to check the model before fitting, and then the section's fit button.

Options and controls

Isotropic (Gauss/Lor)

Add Peak / Remove Peak / Clear All
Edit the peak table.
Auto-Pick Peaks / Pick from Plot
Find peaks automatically, or click them on the plot. Pick on slice chooses which slice of a pseudo-2D to pick from.
Fit only inside the regions
Restricts the fit to your integration regions.
Bound peak amplitudes by region integrals
Keeps each peak's amplitude consistent with the integral of its region.
Across a pseudo-2D series
A relaxation or diffusion series is the same peaks measured repeatedly, so only their intensity should change. Fix shift & FWHM from one slice fits one good Anchor slice and holds position and width for the rest; the alternatives fit every slice independently or seed each from the previous one.
Advanced (fit tolerance and S/N)
S/N threshold, noise region, position tolerance and minimum prominence for automatic picking.
Show Parameter Correlations
Shows the correlation matrix of the fitted parameters. Values near ±1 mean two parameters trade off against each other.

SIMPSON spectrum

Fits a measured spectrum as a sum of SIMPSON simulations, used as fixed lineshape templates. Send a spectrum from Simulate ▸ SIMPSON import, or add one here.

1. SIMPSON spectra
Add selected experiment or Add SIMPSON file… builds the list of templates; Remove takes one out.
2. Fit
Each template always gets its own amplitude, and a baseline is fitted with them. Tick Each template may shift (with a Shift window ±) to allow for referencing or isotropic-shift differences, and Each template may broaden to add Lorentzian and Gaussian broadening. These two widths are partly degenerate, and the errors say so when they are. Amplitudes cannot be negative and Only inside the Integration regions restrict the fit. Fit runs it, and Copy notebook code gives the same call for a notebook.
3. Result
A table of each template's amplitude, shift, Lorentzian and Gaussian broadening, each with its uncertainty, and its share of the total.

CSA, Quad & J-coupling

Peaks (sites) — Add peak, Duplicate, Remove
One row per site. Add isotope partner adds a second isotope of the same chemistry; Same chemistry as shares parameters between peaks.
Add interaction
Adds a CSA, a quadrupolar coupling or a J-coupling to the selected peak. A peak with none is an isotropic peak.
Observe / Spin I / Larmor frequency or Field B₀
The nucleus and field. The Larmor frequency can be typed, or taken from the spectrum.
Regime, MAS rate
Static powder, or finite MAS with sidebands. Fit anisotropic interactions with at least 3 to 5 spinning sidebands for accuracy.
Quadrupolar theory
Second-order perturbation, exact diagonalisation, or first-order only.
Powder averaging / Orientations
The powder-average scheme (ZCW, rectangular or Lebedev) and its number of orientations, with a coarser setting for fitting and a finer one for the final result.
Parameters
Start values, Free tick boxes, and minimum and maximum bounds for every parameter. A blank bound means the natural limit.
Fit window
The whole spectrum, a ppm range, several ppm ranges, or your integration regions.
Estimate Starting Values
Reads starting values from the data in the fit window: peaks by peak picking, a CSA or quadrupolar site from the pattern's centre of mass and shape.
Strongly coupled peaks
For J-coupled spin systems whose shifts are comparable to their couplings (roofing, AB, ABX), solves the spin system exactly instead of using first-order multiplets.
Send to Simulate
Sends the fitted model to Simulate as a starting point.

Other sections

Quadrupolar (Czjzek distribution)
Fits Czjzek (or extended Czjzek) distributions of the quadrupolar coupling to the selected 1D spectrum. For disordered sites in glasses and amorphous solids. Show Distribution Map draws the fitted (CQ, ηQ) distribution beside the spectrum.
pNMR (CIF bond pathways)
Fits a paramagnetic spectrum with the environments predicted from a crystal structure (CIF) and its bond-pathway shifts. The structural model is set up in Simulate ▸ pNMR (CIF). It offers a maximal fit (one peak per group of environments), then Simplify and refit to merge crowded peaks.
Multi-spectrum
Fits one set of sites to several spectra consistently: a series (VT or time), each seeded from the previous fit; isotopes of one material (for example 1H and 2H); or one isotope at several fields. You choose what the x value of each spectrum is (temperature, time, an acquisition parameter or the experiment number).
Kept fit models
Lists the fits you kept with Keep This Fit…, so you can compare several models of the same spectrum (for example 2-, 3- and 4-site models).

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Start simple. Fit the isotropic peaks first, then use Use Fitted Values as Start in the next model. A good start matters more than a long fit.
  • A MAS spectrum does not determine everything. At fast MAS the central transition fixes δiso, CQ, ηQ, J and the site ratio precisely, but hardly the CSA, because the sidebands hold under 1% of the intensity. Fix the CSA there. A static (or slow-MAS) spectrum determines the CSA once the quadrupolar parameters are held at their MAS values. This two-step route is the standard experimental strategy and the fit should follow it.
  • Static powder lineshapes have several minima. A fit started from the wrong sign of the anisotropy can settle in a wrong minimum. The panel's Try starts option fits from several starting points and keeps the best.
  • The sign of CQ is not in a lineshape: it depends on CQ², so |CQ| is reported. When a central transition is poorly resolved, δiso and CQ are hard to separate. The report gives the centre of gravity and PQ = CQ√(1 + ηQ²/3), which are well determined even then; a second field separates the two.
  • A good R² is not proof. Read the warnings in the report, and check Show Parameter Correlations for pairs that trade off.
  • In a notebook. The same fitters are available as NMRCSAFitWorkflow, NMRQuadFitWorkflow, NMRJCouplingFitWorkflow and fit_czjzek_spectrum. Several panels have a Copy notebook code button.
