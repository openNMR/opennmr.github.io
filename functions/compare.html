Compare

Compare lets you view several spectra together, normalise them fairly, and combine them by addition or subtraction.

What it does

Compare puts several spectra in one figure: stacked (one panel each), superimposed on one axis, or as a heatmap with one row per spectrum. It scales them so the comparison is fair, and its Spectrum Arithmetic section adds, subtracts, averages or scales spectra to make a new one, for instance a difference spectrum.

Before you start

Load at least two spectra with Open. They appear in the Open experiments list; Ctrl-click or Shift-click several of them to start a comparison. For a pseudo-2D spectrum, its slices are used.

How to use it

  1. Click Compare in the toolbar and select two or more 1D spectra in Open experiments. They are listed in the Compare list with a tick box each.
  2. Choose a Layout: Stacked, Superimposed or Heatmap. Use Move Up and Move Down to set the order, and the tick boxes (or Select All and Deselect All) to choose which spectra are included. Remove (or the Delete key) takes a spectrum out of the list altogether, and Clear empties it.
  3. Choose how to Normalize them so that intensities can be compared (see below). The plot redraws on every change; Plot redraws it on demand.
  4. To make a new spectrum from the ones ticked, open Spectrum Arithmetic, choose an Operation and click Combine → New Spectrum. The result is added to Open experiments.

Options and controls

Layout
Stacked: one panel per spectrum. Superimposed: all on one axis. Heatmap: one row per spectrum, colour showing intensity, in list order.
Normalize
Per scan (÷ NS), Max intensity, or Per scan and sample mass. Bruker, Varian, Spinsolve and X-Pulse data are divided by the number of scans when loaded; JEOL data are not.
Also divide by receiver gain (RG)
No reader divides by the receiver gain, so spectra recorded with different RG are not comparable in absolute intensity. Tick this to divide each by its RG.
Remove / Clear
Take spectra out of the comparison entirely, not just untick them. A removed spectrum stays out when you change the selection in Open experiments; to bring it back, deselect and reselect it there, or drag it back into the list. Right-click ▸ Remove does the same. The controls above Spectrum Arithmetic sit in one collapsible Compare Controls section.
Fit Curve…
Fits a relaxation or diffusion model across the ticked spectra. See Dynamics.

2D spectra

Overlay / Side by side
Overlay draws every ticked 2D on one set of axes, one colour each (2 to 6 spectra with the same nuclei). Side by side gives each its own panel in a row, column or grid.
Link panels
Zooming or panning one panel moves them all.
Projections on each panel
Adds the F1 and F2 projections beside each side-by-side panel.
Lowest contour / Contour levels
Set the contour range.
Insets
Zoom the plot onto a region, then Add inset from zoom. Choose the panel, the corner and the size, and whether to draw the box and connectors.

Spectrum Arithmetic

Operation
Sum, Average, Subtract from first, or Custom, where you edit the sign and factor of each spectrum yourself.
Range
Overlap only (recommended) uses just the range every spectrum covers, so nothing is invented where a spectrum has no data. Union, zero-filled keeps the whole range.
Align on
ppm (recommended) is field-independent, so it is right for spectra from different fields or carriers. Hz assumes a single SFO1.
Points / Interpolation
The number of points on the common axis, and how each spectrum is resampled onto it. Linear is safest on noisy data; cubic can ring beside sharp peaks.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Intensities look wrong? Check Normalize. Spectra recorded with different numbers of scans, receiver gains or sample masses are not comparable until they are scaled.
  • Nothing happens when you select spectra? Compare needs at least two 1D spectra. The panel says so until you have selected them.
  • Difference spectra. Align the spectra on ppm and use Overlap only, then check that the referencing of the two is consistent. A badly referenced pair leaves derivative-shaped residuals.
