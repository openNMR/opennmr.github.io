Measure T₁ from a series

Process a saturation-recovery series, fit its peaks, then fit T₁.

Data used here: the same lithium sample as the previous tutorial: a saturation-recovery series with echo detection (satrec2Decho3), 16 delays from 10 µs to 60 s, in one Bruker pseudo-2D experiment (16 × 65536 points). The same workflow applies to inversion recovery and to T2 decays.

A relaxation series is the same spectrum recorded 16 times with only the delay changed. openNMR measures it in three steps: process every slice, fit the peaks in each slice (Fit tool), then fit a curve to the peak areas against delay (Dynamics tool).

1. Load and process the series

  1. Open the experiment as before. It appears as a pseudo-2D, “16×65536”. The slice number is in the Phase section.
  2. In Phase, go to slice 16, the fully recovered one, which has the best signal. Keep Apply to every slice ticked so that all slices share one phase.
  3. Click Autophase. openNMR warns that autophasing a whole 2D is slow and suggests extracting one slice, autophasing that, and applying its phase to the source 2D. Do that on a large series: right-click ▸ Extract Slice, autophase the slice, then Apply Phase to Source 2D. A single phase for all slices is the right model here, since only the delay changes.
  4. Open Baseline and click Baseline Correct. It acts on every slice while Apply to every slice is ticked.

2. Fit the peaks in every slice

Open Fit ▸ Isotropic (Gauss/Lor). In a relaxation series the peaks do not move or change width, only their intensity changes, so the sensible way to fit it is to fit one good slice freely and hold its shift and width for all the others.

  1. Under Peaks, add one row per line: here −5.9 ppm (FWHM 8) and −0.25 ppm (FWHM 5). Auto-Pick Peaks (on slice 16) or Pick from Plot also work.
  2. Under Across a pseudo-2D series click Last to anchor on slice 16 (for a T2 decay or a diffusion series you would click First). Leave Across slices on Fix shift & FWHM from one slice. Amplitudes are always free.
  3. Click Fit Spectrum.

The anchor slice fixed the positions at −5.631 and −0.002 ppm, with widths of 7.49 and 4.99 ppm. Use the slice selector (“Spectrum fit (slice #)”) to look at other slices and confirm that the fit still follows the data where the signal is small.

3. Fit T1

  1. Select the Dynamics tool. Under Fit Curve settings set Fit type to Relaxation (T1/T2) and Model to Saturation recovery (T1), which is M(t) = M0(1 − e−t/T1).
  2. Leave Value source on Fitted peak area, the most robust choice if the linewidth changes along the series.
  3. Leave Delays on Auto. openNMR reads the experiment’s own vdlist, and the line below shows the 16 values it found (in the chosen unit). Check them against what you intended.
  4. Click Fit Curve….

4. Read the result

T1 (s)R²
−5.63 ppm2.60 ± 0.020.9999
0.00 ppm2.59 ± 0.010.9969

The ± is 1σ from the scatter of the points about the curve. Four things to check before you report these:

  • The two sites have the same T1 within error. Relaxation therefore cannot tell them apart here. That is a result, not a failure, but it means the two lines should not be described as having different dynamics.
  • The curve must reach its plateau. The last delay is 60 s, about 23 × T1, so the series is fully recovered. A series that stops at 3 × T1 gives an M0 and a T1 that depend on each other.
  • R² = 0.9969 for the second peak is lower than the first. Press Residuals: a systematic pattern means a single exponential is not enough, and you should try the stretched or biexponential recovery from the Model menu.
  • Only two points lie on the plateau, and the early delays (10 µs–2 ms, seven of the sixteen) all have almost zero intensity. The T1 is controlled by the middle of the series. A delay list concentrated between about 0.1 T1 and 3 T1 would probably give the same precision in less time.

Tick Fit all peaks together (shared parameters) to test whether a single shared T1 describes both lines as well as two separate ones do. Export curve fit (CSV)… and Keep This Curve Fit… save the result, and Relaxation vs Temperature carries T1 into an Arrhenius or BPP analysis when you have a temperature series.
